    How I work

    The pattern behind the projects.

    01

    I start with what's missing.

    A lot of my work starts with noticing something that feels unclear, underbuilt, or harder than it needs to be. At 16, it was basketball players who wanted better gear. At BMCC, it was students who didn't have a marketing community. At Leaf Home, it was a regional attribution problem. At Fox, it was a retention issue that needed a clearer structure. The pattern is simple: I notice the gap, figure out why it matters, and build something useful around it.

    02

    I try to understand the full system.

    I don't just want to know my piece of the work. I want to understand how the whole system works, where it breaks, and what decision the work is supposed to support. That's how GeoLink started. It wasn't just a dashboard. It was a way to connect paid media spend, geography, CRM revenue, and budget decisions. That's also how the OutKick retention work started. The question wasn't only "why are users dropping?" It was "what do we actually know, what's missing, and what can the team do with it?"

    03

    I care about making the work useful.

    I like data, but I don't want to report for the sake of reporting. Two years in performance marketing taught me how to read numbers in context: spend, CPA, ROAS, funnel drop-off, geography, creative fatigue, and revenue impact. The goal is not just to explain what happened. The goal is to make the next decision easier.
